# Phase 3 — can interaction structure be inferred for a drug never seen at all?

Every table and figure in this document is generated by
`scripts/report_phase3_entity_ood.py` from `results/phase3_entity_ood/`. CI fails
if a committed table disagrees with the committed metrics.

## 1. The question, and why it is not the one Phase 2R answered

Phase 2R held out **pairs**. Both drugs in a held-out pair still appeared
elsewhere in the training graph, so the model could learn a latent vector for
each from its other partners and then complete the missing entry. That result is
real — Phase 2N established that the Koplev estimator does not manufacture it —
but it is matrix completion. It says nothing about a drug the model has never
measured.

Phase 3 holds out **drugs**:

> Can the interaction behaviour of a drug be predicted when that drug itself has
> never appeared anywhere in training?

For a test drug, no Koplev measurement enters training, validation, feature
preprocessing, target scaling, hyperparameter selection or shrinkage
calibration. The only thing the model knows about it is what the molecule is.

## 2. The target had to change, and the reason is not cosmetic

Phase 2R's target is the residual `D_res` left after subtracting a per-drug
potential `g_i` fitted by ridge from that drug's own Koplev pairs. For a drug
with **no** Koplev pairs in training, that ridge has nothing to fit: the drug's
row and column of the normal equations reduce to `diag(λ)` against `X'y = 0`, so
`a_k = b_k = 0` and `g_k = 0` — **silently, with no error raised**. The
"residual" for a held-out drug would then still contain that drug's entire
potential, and would be a different quantity from the residual for a training
drug. Every skill number computed against it would be partly skill at predicting
a potential nobody removed.

So Phase 3 predicts the raw directional difference

```
D(i, j) = y(i -> j) - y(j -> i),     canonical orientation i < j
```

and the burden Phase 2R discharged by residualisation falls on the model:

```
Dhat(i, j) = g(x_i) - g(x_j) + h(x_i, x_j),     h(x_i, x_j) = -h(x_j, x_i)
```

**This makes the load-bearing comparison a contrast between two models, not a
comparison against zero.** Most of `D`'s energy is per-drug potential. A model
can post a healthy skill against zero while knowing nothing whatsoever about
interaction. The primary quantity is therefore

```
incremental pair skill = 1 - MSE(low-rank) / MSE(feature potential)
```

which is positive only if knowing *which two* drugs are being combined adds
something to knowing what each drug tends to do on its own.

## 6. The two diagnostics that decide what this result means

Neither was pre-registered. Both were added after the primary sweep, and both
are labelled post-hoc throughout — they answer questions the frozen decision rule
does not ask, and they are the reason §10's verdict reads as it does.

### 6a. How much of E1 needs the unseen drug at all?

In E1 the partner is usually a **training** drug (800 of 900 pairs per fold; the
other 100 pair a test drug with a validation drug, which the model has also never
trained on — see §7b). `D(i, j)` contains the partner's ordering tendency with a
minus sign, so a model that knows nothing whatsoever about the new drug still
scores well against zero.

Measuring that floor requires care, and the obvious way is wrong. Zeroing the
held-out drug's feature row looks neutral and is not: it asserts "this drug is a
molecule with zero fingerprint bits", a point no training drug occupies and one
that is systematically *pessimistic*, so anything at all beats it. Against that
baseline the random-feature control scored a spurious +0.052 "attributable to the
unseen drug" at p = 0.049. (An earlier draft justified the zero row by claiming
every head is bias-free; that is true only of the final layer, and the
hidden-layer heads — selected in 13 of 30 potential folds — carry a first-layer
bias, so `g(0) ≠ 0` there.)

The information-free prediction used here is instead the **marginal over the
training drugs**: replace the unseen endpoint by each of the 80 drugs the model
trained on and average. That is the best a model can do knowing only that the
drug is *some* drug from this population, it needs no linearity assumption, and
it is on-distribution by construction. Every representation is shown, including
the controls — a diagnostic is only evidence if features that cannot contain the
answer fail it.

The sharpest form of the question recomputes the **primary contrast itself**
under blinding, with both models blinded identically — so the answer does not
depend on where the baseline sits. If the pair term's advantage needs the actual
molecule, it disappears here; if it is coming from the seen partner, it does not.

**Read the `pair_only` row as vacuous, not as the best result in the table.**
That rung has no potential head, so zeroing the test drug's features sets
`z_i = 0` and the whole prediction to exactly zero — its blind skill is 0.0000 by
construction and its "attributable" column is just its full skill copied across.
The diagnostic only means something for rungs that retain a potential term when
the unseen drug is removed.

The rows that do mean something say the opposite of what was registered:
**the potential model's skill is not significantly attributable to the unseen
drug in either screen** (p = 0.33, 0.57), while the low-rank model's is
(p = 0.020, 0.011). Chemistry does not tell you whether a new drug prefers to go
first. It tells you something about how it interacts with a drug you already
know.

### 6b. Is the low-rank model's prediction a potential, or is it pair-specific?

A rank-2 antisymmetric form **contains the potential exactly**: put
`z_i = (g_i, 1)` and `K = [[0,1],[-1,0]]` and `z_i' K z_j = g_i - g_j`. So
beating the potential rung does not by itself demonstrate pair structure — the
bilinear form may simply be fitting a better potential.

The result files show this is a live worry and not a theoretical one: the
`pair_only` rung, which has no potential head at all, is statistically
indistinguishable from the potential rung on held-out E1 — a fold-mean gap of
−0.005 on A375 and +0.017 on PANC1 (paired t p = 0.67 / 0.15), i.e. on PANC1 it
is if anything the higher of the two.

The test is to fit the best per-drug potential **to the model's own predictions**
— using no outcome — and ask what will not fit. The projection is deliberately
generous: 99 free parameters against 900 E1 pairs. Anything it cannot absorb is
not a potential. The positive-control rows are the calibration, since there the
target really does contain a planted rank-2 term.

## 7. Does it survive for drugs far from the training set?

Strata at the pre-registered cut points (`low < 0.234 <= medium < 0.516 <= high`),

## 7b. What E1 is actually made of

E1 is **not** homogeneous, and every earlier draft of this document said it was.

training pair either. So of the 900 E1 rows in a fold, 800 are test × trained and
**100 are test × untrained**: a second both-unseen regime sitting inside the
primary one. Since this phase's whole reinterpretation rests on what the partner

### What these three readings mean, in order

**1. The frozen rule returns INCONCLUSIVE, and the reason is not leakage.**

Its validity gate reads skill-against-zero on the control representations, and
both controls clear it. But in E1 one endpoint is a *training* drug, and
`D(i, j)` carries that drug's ordering tendency with a minus sign, so a model
that knows nothing whatsoever about the new drug still scores well against zero.
This is not an argument, it is a measurement. Compared on the *same ten folds*,
**random feature vectors containing no chemistry at all beat real fingerprints in
three of the four screen × rung cells** — including both screens for the
potential rung, where random reaches +0.2332 / +0.1761 against ECFP4's +0.1973 /
+0.1493. A statistic on which noise outperforms signal cannot distinguish the two
things that gate was written to distinguish. The choice was made before the
regime was understood, and it was the wrong one.

**2. Correcting that single statistic gives PAIR-SPECIFIC ENTITY TRANSFER.**

`verdict_posthoc` substitutes the primary endpoint — incremental pair skill — for
skill-against-zero in criterion (f) and its gate, and changes nothing else. On
that statistic the controls collapse exactly as intended: random −0.0044 /
+0.0030, shuffled −0.0031 / −0.0001, against real fingerprints' +0.0273 /
+0.0536.

**3. The classification reported is ANALOGUE-ONLY TRANSFER, and reading 2 passes
only on a technicality of my own drafting.**

Criterion (g) — "not analogue-confined" — as pre-registered asked only that the
low-similarity stratum's mean incremental skill be **> 0**, in one screen, with
no significance requirement. It is +0.0160, so it passes. That is a bar I wrote
too low, before it was clear how much of E1 the seen partner supplies.

What the stratification actually shows, per held-out drug: **+0.016 (p = 0.13)
and +0.015 (p = 0.22) among the 40 chemically distant drugs, ≈ 0 in the middle
third, and +0.090 (p = 0.0024) / +0.138 (p = 0.00029) among the 31 with a close
training analogue.** High versus low differs at Mann-Whitney p = 0.0089 / 0.0011.

The shape is a **step at the top, not a gradient** — the middle third sits at or
below the low third in both screens, and among the 69 drugs below the high cut
there is no trend at all. The full-sample Spearman ρ of +0.23 / +0.32 is carried
entirely by the top stratum, and under a bootstrap that resamples congener
families rather than drugs it survives on PANC1 and not on A375. That is the
definition of analogue-only: the method interpolates across close chemical
analogues and has not been shown to extrapolate.

Reporting reading 2 as the answer would be letting a criterion I wrote badly
overrule the evidence it was meant to test.

### What is nevertheless established

Three things survive, and they are not nothing.

* **The pair term's advantage requires the unseen drug's own structure.** This is
  the sharpest test available and the one the conclusion rests on: recompute the
  primary contrast with *both* models blinded to the held-out drug, by replacing
  it with the marginal over the 80 training drugs. The incremental pair skill
  falls from **+0.0273 to +0.0052 (A375) and from +0.0536 to +0.0052 (PANC1)** —
  differences of +0.0222 (p = 0.014) and +0.0484 (p = 0.00038), both CIs
  excluding zero. **No control reproduces it**: random features −0.003 / +0.006,
  shuffled fingerprints −0.001 / −0.002, shuffled targets −0.001 / −0.001, all
  n.s. The planted-signal positive control loses +0.372 (p = 3 × 10⁻⁵), which is
  what the diagnostic looks like when the answer really is a function of the
  features.
* **The pair term is doing pair-specific work, not refitting the potential.**
  A rank-2 antisymmetric form contains the potential exactly, so this had to be
  checked. The low-rank model's predictions carry a curl component no potential
  can express (share 0.135 / 0.184), and the skill it gains over its own gradient
  projection (+0.020 / +0.049) accounts for essentially all of its advantage over
  the potential rung. The shuffled control gains nothing from its curl.
* **The advantage is analogue-confined.** Concentrated in the 31 drugs with a
  close training analogue; absent, within the resolution available, among the 40
  chemically distant ones. Under a bootstrap that resamples congener families
  rather than drugs, high-minus-low survives on both screens (p = 0.047 / 0.015)
  while the similarity *gradient* survives only on PANC1 (p = 0.117 / 0.038) —
  and below the high cut the correlation is if anything negative on both.

### What does not survive, and was claimed in an earlier draft

* **"Structure predicts a new drug's interactions but not its potential."** That
  rested on a per-model blind diagnostic whose baseline was a zeroed feature row,
  which is systematically pessimistic rather than uninformative — against it the
  random-feature control scored a spurious +0.052 (p = 0.049). With the
  training-drug marginal as the baseline, the "attributable to the unseen drug"
  figures for the primary run are **−0.015 (p = 0.35) and −0.006 (p = 0.70)** for
  the potential model and **+0.004 (p = 0.83) and +0.034 (p = 0.12)** for the
  low-rank model. Neither is significant, and the claim is withdrawn. What
  replaces it is the blinded-contrast result above, which is a within-pair
  comparison and does not depend on where the baseline sits.
* **"E2 fails cleanly ... nothing transfers."** No E2 rung differs significantly
  from zero (p = 0.07–0.38, 12–17 of 30 folds positive), the pair-pooled skills
  are −0.010 to +0.007 rather than the −0.04 to −0.09 the fold means suggest, and
  at 45 pairs per fold the arm is powered only to +0.09…+0.14 — it could have
  detected nothing short of E1-magnitude transfer. "No transfer detected" is what
  the data support.

  The stronger evidence on this point is not E2 at all but **§7b**: the 100 E1
  pairs per fold whose partner is a *validation* drug — untrained on, like the
  test drug — score +0.007 / +0.034 against +0.215 / +0.153 on the 800 pairs with
  a trained partner. Ten times the sample size of E2, and the same answer.

### Limitations that are not resolved here

* 30 folds come from 3 partitions of the same 100 drugs, so they are not fully
  independent; the per-drug analysis (n = 100, each averaging 3 folds) is the
  more conservative unit and gives the same picture.
* The low-similarity stratum is *not significant*, which is not the same as
  *zero*. With n = 40 and the observed spread it is powered (80%, α = 0.05) to
  detect **+0.029 / +0.032** — roughly a third of the high-stratum effect. So an
  effect as large as the analogues' would certainly have shown (power ≈ 1.0);
  the observed +0.016 / +0.015 is about half the detectable floor, and an effect
  genuinely that small could not be resolved here either way.
* ECFP4 is one representation, deliberately the simplest defensible one. A
  negative result for chemically distant drugs is a result about ECFP4 at this
  sample size, not about chemistry.
* The four metal compounds have near-degenerate fingerprints; excluding them
  changes nothing (+0.0267 / +0.0548 against +0.0273 / +0.0536).
