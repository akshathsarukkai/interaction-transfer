# Phase 3 — drug identity: mapping 100 Koplev labels to chemical structures

Every number in this document is generated by `scripts/audit_phase3_drugs.py`
from the committed mapping. None is transcribed from an auditor's report: an
independent audit is *evidence about* the mapping, not a source of numbers to
copy, and the mapping changed as a result of it.

## Why this is the highest-risk step in Phase 3

Phase 3 asks whether a drug's structure predicts its interaction behaviour. A
mis-mapped label does not make the experiment noisy in a way that widens a
confidence interval — it silently answers a different question, and it makes a
null result unfalsifiable ("maybe the mapping was bad"). So every decision here
is made by a rule fixed in advance and applied uniformly, never by looking at a
compound and judging it, and the two databases are queried independently so that
agreement is evidence rather than assumption.

## The source

The deposit's labels are the compound names of the **NCI DTP Approved Oncology
Drugs Set IV** (Developmental Therapeutics Program, NCI/DCTD), which is the
library Koplev et al. state they screened. Several strings carry a vendor-style
salt suffix (`Erlotinib HCl`, `Imatinib Mesylate`) and five carry a second name
after a semicolon or in parentheses.

**The deposit contains no compound-identifier table**, and neither does the
paper's supplement — checked directly against the Mendeley listing for
`wgybvcvjwf` v1, which holds exactly the five CSVs recorded in
`data/raw/koplev2017/PROVENANCE.json`. So there is no source-side identifier list
to map against; identity has to be established from the names.

## The policy

1. **Normalisation.** Text before the first `;`, parenthesised groups deleted,
   whitespace collapsed. Nothing else. Salt words are deliberately *kept* —
   desalting is a structural operation, not a string one.
2. **Resolution.** PubChem by name → CID, using **that CID's structure**.
3. **Desalting.** Keep the largest covalent fragment, and only if what is kept is
   at least twice the largest discarded fragment by heavy-atom count.
4. **Metals.** A molecule containing a metal or metalloid is kept **whole**.
5. **Protonation.** Neutralised with RDKit's `Uncharger` (metals exempt).
6. **Cross-check.** ChEMBL by preferred name → `molecule_hierarchy` parent → same
   desalting → compare InChIKey connectivity blocks.

### Why not PubChem's own parent relation

It was tried first and fails silently, twice in this set of 100. On
**carboplatin** it returns 1,1-cyclobutanedicarboxylic acid — the platinum is
discarded and the drug would have entered as an unrelated small diacid. On
**tamoxifen citrate** it returns the citrate salt unchanged, because that record
has no registered parent, so the fingerprint would have carried citric acid.

The fragment rule replaces it because its failure mode is *inspectable*: the
discarded fragments are recorded, and nothing may be discarded that is more than
half the size of what is kept. The tightest case in the deposit is, fittingly,
tamoxifen citrate — citric acid is 13 heavy atoms against tamoxifen's 28, a ratio
of 2.15, clearing the rule by 0.15.

### Why a regex on salt words would be wrong

`Pemetrexed Disodium` is a salt and must lose its sodiums, while
`Fludarabine Phosphate`, `Estramustine phosphate` and `Megestrol acetate` are
covalent esters and prodrugs whose "salt-looking" suffix must be **kept** — the
screen dosed those molecules, and they are different compounds from their
alcohols. A fragment rule gets this right for free: a covalent ester is one
fragment and survives intact.

### Why metals are exempt

RDKit writes cisplatin as `N.N.Cl[Pt]Cl`: three fragments, none of which is the
drug. Arsenic trioxide is `[O-2].[O-2].[O-2].[As+3].[As+3]` — five fragments and
no organic part at all. In a coordination complex the "counterion" is the
pharmacology.

## The independent audit

Eighteen independent auditors: ten covering ten drugs each and re-deriving every
identity from primary sources without assuming the mapping was right, and eight
applying specialist lenses across all 100 (same-name-different-compound,
salt/prodrug, stereochemistry, abbreviations, database disagreement, near
duplicates, Koplev provenance, fingerprint degeneracy). Every finding was then
handed to a **separate** adjudicator instructed to refute it and to default to
rejection. 28 raw findings, 14 distinct after merging, **4 upheld**.

### Upheld and acted on

**Carboplatin — material, and it reached the features.** PubChem's name-indexed
record CID 426756 — the only hit for the literal name — mis-depicts the two
ammine (NH₃) ligands as azanide anions `[NH2-]` and the chelating
cyclobutane-1,1-dicarboxylate dianion as the neutral free diacid. The molecular
formula is coincidentally identical (C₆H₁₂N₂O₄Pt), so the formula check cannot
see it; CHEMBL1351 carries no structure at all, so the cross-check could not
fire. Formal charge and hydrogen count are both Morgan atom invariants, so this
is not a depiction detail: ECFP4 Tanimoto between the two forms is 0.50, and the
mis-depicted one is **0.875 similar to bare 1,1-cyclobutanedicarboxylic acid**.

Rejecting PubChem's parent relation had stopped carboplatin entering as an
unrelated small diacid; PubChem's own *name* record then delivered a fingerprint
87.5% similar to that diacid by another route. Overridden to CID 10339178,
corroborated through UniChem by DrugBank DB00958, ChEBI:31355 and FDA UNII
BG3F62OND5.

**The general lesson is narrower than "check the structures".** ChEMBL deposits
no structure for any of the four metal compounds, so the cross-check that guards
the other 96 drugs is silently absent for exactly the four whose depiction is
hardest.

**Pentostatin — minor.** Deposited as the 4H tautomer where the FDA label,
CHEMBL1580 and PDB component DCF give the 3,6,7,8-tetrahydro form. Invisible to
every check in the pipeline, because standard InChI treats that proton as mobile
and both forms give the same key. Overridden to ChEMBL's structure.

**Vincristine — minor, and provably wrong rather than a matter of preference.**
The resolved record is the C-15 epimer. The proof is internal to this mapping:
vincristine is N1′-desmethyl-N1′-formyl-vinblastine and differs from vinblastine
at no other atom, so applying that single substitution to *this mapping's own
vinblastine* must reproduce vincristine — and it yields
`OGWKCGZFUXNPDA-CFWMRBGOSA-N`, not the recorded key. Both databases carry the
same legacy structure, and the connectivity block is identical for two epimers.
Overridden to CID 5388993. **This correction does not reach the features**, since
the primary fingerprint uses `useChirality=False`; it is applied so the stored
structure is right.

**Docstring provenance — documentation.** The labels were described as Selleck
catalogue names. They are NCI DTP Approved Oncology Drugs Set IV names, as
`docs/phase2_dataset.md` already recorded.

### The rule for overrides, and the case that proves it bites

An override may only point at **another deposited record in an authoritative
database** — never at a hand-drawn structure, however good the reasoning.
Without that constraint this table becomes the place where the mapping is
adjusted until it looks right.

The constraint bites immediately. The audit found the *same* defect in
**oxaliplatin** — CID 9887053 writes neutral oxalic acid plus two amide anions —
and no database holds a corrected depiction: the correct (1R,2R)-DACH /
oxalate(2−) / Pt(II) structure has no PubChem CID at all
(`ZROHGHOFXNOHSO-BNTLRKBRSA-L` returns no hit; the nearest record, CID 15057790,
is the wrong enantiomer). **Oxaliplatin is therefore not overridden.** The defect
is recorded here, and the pre-registered metal-excluded arm is what answers it.

### Raised and rejected

Ten findings did not survive adjudication. The substantive ones: the three
PubChem/ChEMBL disagreements (**plicamycin**, **streptozocin**, **arsenic
trioxide**) are all same-formula, same-heavy-atom-count curation differences
where the ChEMBL record is the defective side, not wrong-drug errors;
**bleomycin**'s partially-undefined stereochemistry is a property of the deposited
record for a natural-product mixture, not a mapping error; **mitomycin C** and
**thioguanine** are correct and their flags are false negatives of the ChEMBL
name lookup and of tautomer convention respectively.

## What the representation actually looks like

ECFP4 (radius 2, 2048 bits, `useChirality=False`) over the corrected structures.

The four metal compounds are the honest weak point: arsenic trioxide sets **2**
bits and cisplatin **5**, against a median of 44.5. No amount of structural
correctness makes a Morgan fingerprint a good description of a coordination
complex. That is why the pre-registration includes a metal-excluded re-scoring of
every primary fit, and why that arm is a *sensitivity* result rather than a
second headline.

The set is chemically diverse, which is what makes "analogue-only transfer" a
hypothesis that can fail here rather than a foregone conclusion. Median pairwise
Tanimoto is 0.077; 246 of the 4,950 pairs are exactly zero; **no pair reaches
0.9**, and only eight reach 0.7. Those eight are all genuine close-analogue drug
pairs, not mapping errors:

## Secondary representation: curated targets

ChEMBL's `mechanism` table, queried by `parent_molecule_chembl_id` — not
`molecule_chembl_id`, which returns nothing for erlotinib, imatinib, lapatinib,
tamoxifen, doxorubicin, vinblastine and eighteen others, because ChEMBL registers
mechanisms against the *drug* record, which for a salt is the salt. The wrong
field gives 75/100 coverage that looks plausible until you notice the missing
drugs are the famous ones. The correct field gives 97/100.

Its limit, decided on the annotation and not on any result: **16 of 100 drugs
have a target vector orthogonal to every other drug** (3 unannotated, 13 whose
every target is unique to them) and cannot transfer by construction. That is a
property of the annotation's granularity, and it is why this arm is secondary.

## Reproduction

```
python scripts/prepare_phase3_drugs.py     # PubChem + ChEMBL -> mapping CSV
python scripts/prepare_phase3_targets.py   # ChEMBL mechanisms -> target CSV
python scripts/audit_phase3_drugs.py       # regenerate every number above
```

Raw API responses are cached under `data/external/cache/` (gitignored) and
digested into `data/external/PROVENANCE_PHASE3.json`, so the mapping is
reproducible from this repository even if a database re-curates a record.
