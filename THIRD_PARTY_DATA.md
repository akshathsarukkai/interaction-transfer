## What is and is not redistributed

### Not redistributed

No raw third-party dataset is committed to this repository. `data/raw/` and
`third_party/` are gitignored, and every acquisition path records a SHA-256
digest of what it fetched, so the exact bytes a result was computed from are
pinned even though the bytes themselves are not here.

That policy predates this public release; it is the policy the private research
repository already ran under, and it is kept unchanged. It is stricter than the
licences strictly require in one case — the Koplev deposit is CC BY 4.0 and
*could* be redistributed — and the reason is given in
`scripts/download_koplev.py`: a reproducible fetch plus a recorded digest is a
better provenance record than a copy, because a copy silently stops matching
upstream while a digest check fails loudly.

### Redistributed, and why it is safe to

**Derived identifier mappings.** `data/external/koplev_drug_mapping.csv` and
`data/external/koplev_drug_targets.csv` are small tables of factual
identifiers — compound names, PubChem CIDs, InChIKeys, SMILES, ChEMBL target
IDs and mechanism strings — generated by the two committed scripts named above
from public web services. They are included because the committed Phase 3
results are not checkable without them, and because they are what the drift
checks in CI regenerate `docs/phase3_drug_mapping.md` from. Their provenance,
including SHA-256 digests over the cached API responses they were built from, is
in `data/external/PROVENANCE_PHASE3.json` and
`data/external/PROVENANCE_PHASE3_TARGETS.json`. Both are regenerable from
scratch; neither reproduces a substantial portion of any upstream database.

**Test fixtures are generated, not sampled.** `tests/fixtures/koplev_tiny/` and
`tests/fixtures/chemlex_tiny/` reproduce the deposits' exact column schemas and
none of their content. Both fixture generators say so in their own docstrings and
both are pinned by CI, which regenerates each fixture and fails if the committed
copy differs. The ChemLex fixture's molecules are commercially trivial reagents
(acetic acid, benzoic acid, aniline, morpholine) chosen so RDKit exercises real
chemistry; they are not drawn from the deposit. This matters specifically because
the ChemLex deposit is CC BY-NC 4.0: a fixture sampled from it could not be
published, and this one is not sampled from it.

**Results are this project's own output.** Everything under `results/` was
produced by the code in this repository and is covered by `LICENSE`.

### The ChemLex substrate inventory, and the two places a fragment of it remains

An earlier state of this work committed a `smiles` column in five per-entity
result CSVs. Across them it carried **497 of the deposit's 503 distinct reactant
structures** — 98.9% of the acids and 98.7% of the amines. That is the deposit's
substrate inventory in all but name, in a repository that states in six places
that the deposit is not ours to vendor. The column has been removed from every
committed artifact, the writers in `chemlex/sweep.py` and
`scripts/report_phase4_chemlex.py` now drop it on the way out, and a CI step
fails the build if it reappears. Nothing downstream read it: `role` plus `entity`
is the key every table joins on, and a reader who has fetched the deposit gets
the structures re-attached locally.

Two small, purposive quotations remain, and they are named here rather than left
for a reader to find:

- **`docs/phase4_chemlex_mapping.md`** lists every merged split group with its
  members — about 40 structures, 8% of the inventory. They are the entities the
  grouping logic *merged*, and the table exists so a reader can check that
  decision. Removing it would mean asserting a data-integrity fix without
  showing it.
- **`tests/test_phase4_chemlex.py`** names four structures: Fmoc-Lys(Dde)-OH
  drawn as the imine and the enaminone, and valsartan with its tetrazole drawn
  1H and 2H. These are the two acid pairs that were the same compound on
  opposite sides of a fold — one of the three defects that forced a corrected
  re-run. A regression test for a specific compound has to name that compound.

Both are quotations for verification of a documented correction, not
redistribution of the dataset, and both are a small fraction of it. They are
recorded here so that the judgement is visible and reviewable rather than
implicit.

## The d-chain relationship, stated precisely

Phase 2N tests whether the low-rank cyclic signature seen in the Koplev data
could be manufactured by the estimator that produced that data. Doing that
honestly requires running *the published sampler itself*, not a reimplementation
of it. So:

- The upstream GPL-3.0 source is **not vendored**. `scripts/prepare_dchain_null.py`
  fetches it at a pinned commit, verifies every file digest, applies the patches
  enumerated in `dchain_null/dchain.py`, compiles it, and then refuses the build
  unless the patched program reproduces the unpatched one byte for byte on the
  authors' own example data at the published seed.
- `src/intervention_algebra/real_data/dchain_null/synergy.py` implements the
  authors' synergy measure. Its docstring **quotes roughly ten lines of the
  upstream R** (`post/interpretMCMC.R`, `summaryStatisticsMCMC()`) for
  verification, with attribution, because every claim the experiment makes rests
  on the port being exact. The Python is this project's own expression of the
  same measure; the upstream code is quoted, not copied wholesale.
- `src/intervention_algebra/real_data/dchain_null/dchain.py` embeds roughly
  **27 lines of the upstream C++** as the `old` match strings of its patch
  table, each paired with an edited `new` string. A patch that does not match
  its target exactly is a patch that silently does nothing, so the fragments are
  quoted rather than described — the byte-equivalence check that licenses the
  whole falsification is only meaningful if every edit is checkable against the
  line it edits.
- Both files carry an explicit upstream copyright and licence notice in their
  module docstrings. Those quoted fragments are covered by GPL-3.0, not by this
  repository's Apache-2.0 grant, and the `LICENSE` file does not purport to
  relicense them.
- Anyone redistributing the d-chain source itself, or building a work that
  incorporates it, is bound by GPL-3.0 and should obtain the source from
  <https://github.com/skoplev/d-chain> under those terms.

This boundary is documented rather than glossed because it is the one place where
this project's Apache-2.0 code sits next to GPL-3.0 upstream work, and because
the honest description of it is "quoted with attribution for verification",
which is a judgement rather than a certainty. If you intend to redistribute a
combined work, read the upstream licence yourself and take your own advice on
it. The two files concerned are
`src/intervention_algebra/real_data/dchain_null/dchain.py` and
`src/intervention_algebra/real_data/dchain_null/synergy.py`; nothing else in
this repository derives from GPL-3.0 code.
